Values are printed exactly as published. Indian brokerage is usually conditional — “₹20 or 0.03%, whichever is lower” — and reducing that to a single number would misstate the price. A ~ marks a value read out of prose rather than a pricing table: the broker renders its pricing in JavaScript, so it is less reliable than the rest. Tickers link through to that company's page in the intelligence terminal.

These are set by regulation, not by the broker: securities transaction tax, exchange transaction charges, GST, the SEBI turnover fee and state stamp duty. They are shown separately on purpose — folding them into a single “total cost” makes brokers look far more alike than they are, and makes a zero-brokerage broker look expensive.

Method

Collected by ingest/92_broker_charges.py from each broker's own pricing page, never from an affiliate comparison site — those are monetised by referral and go stale quietly. robots.txt was checked for every host before fetching. Each run stamps the date and appends rather than overwrites, so rate changes accumulate as history — which is where the value is over time, since brokerage rates are the revenue line for the listed brokers here.
